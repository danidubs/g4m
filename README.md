# g4m
g4m - grid 4 magic image sound / where math meets random, and random fills formulas / light with an address

g4m is not a tool for making art — g4m itself is the artwork. A conceptual piece: an app that pours out an immeasurable flood of images and sound, worldwide, and in doing so holds up a mirror to how inflationary our handling of images — and by now of sound — has become.
The images are not artworks, and neither is the sound. Deliberately «sound», not «music»: nothing that leaves the app springs from an intuitive, creative act. Images and sound grow from mathematical formulas fed with random numbers. Every press of Go raises one from a formula on a grid, stirred with a controlled dose of chance. The superformula alone can produce more images — each one unique — than there are stars in the universe. So what is all this, if it is not art? It is output: the work’s yield — endless, weightless, and never the work itself.

The creative act lives one level higher, in the code. Writing it was an intuitive process, steered by nothing but the artist’s eye and ear — an intense, exhilarating phase of creation, as consuming as any studio work. The work is the code — all of it, in one single HTML file. That is the point. g4m is deliberately a monolith: splitting it into modules would be like sawing the marble block into pieces and gluing Michelangelo’s David back together from them. One file of some three megabytes, everything embedded — the typefaces, the images, the texts, the entire sound engine. A single, temporary exception: the small construction-site icon still lives outside the file, and it will disappear the day the code is declared final. The code is one hundred percent public; only the authorship remains with the artist alone.

The claim says everything the work is: grid 4 magic image sound. The grid is the artist’s most important design decision. It is the common language between artist and machine: nearly all shaping is negotiated in grid units, u by u (u is the one base unit of the interface raster — every width, height and position in g4m is a multiple of it), so unambiguous that misunderstandings hardly ever arise. And the same grid is the coordinate system on which the formulas draw their graphs. Four words — grid, magic, image, sound — are never translated: they need no translation, whatever languages the app may come to speak (today four — a very Swiss number — tomorrow perhaps more). And they carry a quiet numerology: magic, image and sound have five letters each; grid has four — the same 4 that stands in the middle of the claim, readable as «for» and as the count of its own letters. The one four carries the fives. Coincidence? The artist keeps wondering.

HTML is the canvas here. Since HTML5 the language has carried an element literally called canvas — and it was this canvas that turned an old spreadsheet obsession into g4m: what once had to be coaxed out of Excel tables filled with random numbers now unfolds in every browser in the world. The same language that pours out the flood of images and sound also builds and shapes this interface — itself a small artwork: a tool for exploring the world of mathematical chance playfully.
